# Photo-only product and visual brief

**Only source:** the six original app screenshots Ref-1.png–Ref-6.png (each 1284 × 2778 portrait). This brief describes visible content only. It deliberately does not mix in later gameplay requests, inferred physics or generic game-design assumptions.

## Product appearance
A playful vintage-aircraft distance game. The playable scenes combine an exposed orange/tan wire-braced aircraft, a large striped-elastic launcher, and a sparse stylized low-poly desert. A colourful mobile-game HUD and upgrade menu sit over the 3D scenes. The loading artwork is a separate glossy toy-room diorama featuring a more elaborate biplane.

## Screen composition
- Portrait format, approximately 0.462 width-to-height.
- Aircraft and forward world dominate interactive views.
- Flight HUD sits near the edges; central route remains open.
- Launch camera is centred behind the aircraft, slightly elevated, looking over the wing toward the ramp.
- Flight camera follows directly behind and above the aircraft, looking forward/down. Aircraft stays in the lower-middle while terrain fills the view ahead.
- Aircraft is large: main wing occupies approximately 67–75% of width in Ref-5 and 80–86% in Ref-6 (visual estimates).
- Do not substitute side-on, cockpit, overhead-map or distant tiny-aircraft framing.

## Playable aircraft
Match Ref-3 through Ref-6:
- Broad shallow-chord wing, mostly rectangular with softened ends.
- Warm orange/honey/ochre surface divided into rectangular panels by darker orange-brown ribs/grid.
- Thin dark bracing wires from central structure to several points across both wing halves.
- Long narrow pale-timber/tan exposed fuselage or tail boom with repeated diagonal lattice bracing.
- Small dark open cockpit/seat recess near wing root.
- Smaller orange panelled horizontal tailplane at the rear, with left/right surfaces and a thin rear extension.
- Thin structural members, dark fittings and cable attachment points.
- Lightweight vintage/early-flying-machine appearance.

A clear propeller, enclosed canopy, jet engine and conventional landing wheels are not visible on this playable model.

**Do not conflate with Ref-1:** loading art shows a separate wooden-fuselage biplane with two stacked camouflage wings, inter-wing struts, green nose propeller and wheels.

## Slingshot and launch scene
Match Ref-2 through Ref-4:
- Large physical launcher aligned with aircraft centreline.
- Two widely separated upright brown/timber-looking posts at the sides.
- Yellow-and-white diagonally striped elastic bands converge from posts toward aircraft in a broad V.
- Wide dark-brown ramp extends forward, broad near the camera and narrowing in perspective.
- Ref-4 has a segmented semicircular aiming arc across near ramp: red/orange ends, yellow intermediate sections, green centre.
- Ref-2 shows a larger dark central support/tower-like assembly, partly obscured by plane and interface.

The photos do not define launcher internals, elastic force, exact aim range or player gesture.

## Desert world
Ref-3–Ref-6 show an open arid desert:
- Warm beige, tan, ochre and sandy-brown ground.
- Broad rolling sandy slopes, shallow valleys/depressions and open basins.
- Angular/faceted rocky hills and outcrops; a large rocky slope is prominent at right in Ref-6.
- Sparse scattered vegetation: tall branching cacti, shorter clustered cactus forms, leafless thin-trunk trees with angular branches and a few low plants.
- Large open sand areas between vegetation; no continuous grass carpet or dense cloned shrub pattern.
- Small quadruped animal silhouettes at several distances; exact species cannot be identified.
- Bright cyan/light-blue sky; soft white clouds in Ref-5.
- Distant landscape fades gently into pale blue-grey haze; foreground remains clear.
- Bright daylight, warm illumination and soft directional shadows, including a visible aircraft shadow in Ref-5.

## Rendering
- Stylized low-poly 3D, not photorealistic.
- Large polygonal terrain forms, faceted rock planes, simple recognizable vegetation silhouettes.
- Matte-looking sand, restrained surface texture, broad tonal variation and soft shading.
- Clear silhouettes, warm earth colours against blue sky, readable nearby objects and simplified pale distant objects.
- Atmospheric perspective should affect distance only; the references do not show opaque fog obscuring the playable scene.
- Keep open sand and visual breathing room; avoid high-frequency clutter.

Ref-1's glossy toy-room diorama, dramatic depth of field and polished reflections are loading-art treatment, not gameplay rendering.

## Flight HUD
- Upper-left circular speed dial with layered silver/chrome rim, blue/cyan face, tick marks, dark needle and large KM/H value. Ref-5: 94 KM/H; Ref-6: 37 KM/H with red LOW SPEED warning.
- White pause bars inside pale translucent rounded square below the speed dial.
- Bold white distance with dark shadow near centre above aircraft. Ref-5: 167M; Ref-6: 370M.
- Narrow vertical rounded progress track at right, pale/blue fill and light outline, checkered finish marker at top, yellow progress marker and arrow, small white plane marker and percentage. Ref-5: 4%; Ref-6: 9%.
- Ref-5 shows large white PERFECT! with blue outline/dark shadow and green lateral/pale speed streaks. Ref-6 shows LOW SPEED instead.
- HUD stays near edges and does not obscure forward route.

## Visual implementation priority
1. Portrait composition and rear-follow flight camera.
2. Correct playable aircraft silhouette, orange panel grid and exposed lattice construction.
3. Large aircraft framing with visible forward route.
4. Sparse natural low-poly desert: open sand, cacti, bare trees, angular rocks and distant animals.
5. Correct side-post launcher, striped elastic V, ramp and coloured aiming arc.
6. Edge-aligned speed/distance/progress HUD and state feedback.
7. Hangar menu and three upgrade cards.
8. Separate loading-art visual treatment.
